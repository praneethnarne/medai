# Data audit — PAD-UFES-20

Every number here is read from `results/pad_ufes20_audit.json`, generated by
`backend/src/data/audit_pad_ufes.py`. Metadata only; no images were downloaded.

Source: ISIC collection 406. Licence CC-BY. Image type: *clinical: close-up* (smartphone).
Data descriptor: Pacheco et al., *Data in Brief* 32:106221, 2020.

## Shape

**2,298 images · 1,891 lesions · 1,373 patients**

Multiple images per lesion and multiple lesions per patient. **Splits must therefore be
made at the patient level.** An image-level split leaks the same lesion across train and
test and silently inflates every metric. This is the single easiest way to ruin the
project's results and it has to be right from the first split.

| Diagnosis | Images | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Basal cell carcinoma | 845 | 36.8% |
| Solar / actinic keratosis | 730 | 31.8% |
| Nevus | 244 | 10.6% |
| Seborrheic keratosis | 235 | 10.2% |
| Squamous cell carcinoma | 192 | 8.4% |
| **Melanoma** | **52** | **2.3%** |

Grouped by malignancy: Malignant 1,089 · Indeterminate 730 · Benign 479.

## Three findings that change the plan

### 1. Melanoma is almost absent — 52 images

The clinically critical class has 52 images across the whole dataset. After a patient-level
split that leaves roughly **five melanomas in the test set**. A per-class recall computed
on five images carries a confidence interval wide enough to be meaningless, and no amount
of careful methodology fixes that.

Consequence: **the primary task becomes malignancy triage, not fine-grained diagnosis.**
Malignant / indeterminate / benign is well powered (1,089 / 730 / 479) and it is also the
decision the product actually makes — *do you need to see a doctor?* The six-class label is
reported as secondary, informational output.

Melanoma recall is still reported, always with its confidence interval and always with the
support count beside it, so nobody — including us — mistakes it for a reliable number.
HAM10000's 1,113 melanomas remain useful during pretraining.

### 2. The label priors are inverted between the two domains

HAM10000 is dominated by nevi (~67%). PAD-UFES-20 is dominated by basal cell carcinoma
(36.8%) with nevi at 10.6%. So the shift between dermoscopy and phone photos is not only
one of image appearance — it is a large **label-prior shift** as well.

This matters for the cross-domain experiment. A naive model carried from HAM10000 to
PAD-UFES-20 will be wrong partly because the images look different and partly because the
base rates differ. Those two causes must be separated, or the headline finding is
uninterpretable. Prior correction will be applied and reported as its own ablation.

### 3. Skin tone is recorded, and the dataset is still overwhelmingly light

Fitzpatrick type is present for 1,494 of 2,298 images (65%) — HAM10000 has no skin-tone
labels at all, so this is the only place in the project where fairness can be measured.

| Fitzpatrick | I | II | III | IV | V | VI | unrecorded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Images | 153 | 876 | 392 | 62 | 10 | 1 | 804 |

Types V and VI together account for **eleven images**. We can compare performance across
I–III against IV+, and we will. We cannot say anything defensible about dark skin, and we
will not pretend otherwise.

This is worth stating plainly because the reference paper this project departs from cites
misdiagnosis on darker skin as its motivation, then trains on data that cannot support any
claim about it. Naming that limitation honestly is a better contribution than quietly
inheriting it.

## What this means for Block A

- Patient-level stratified splitting, with a committed manifest and a leakage test.
- Primary target: malignancy triage. Secondary: six-class diagnosis.
- Label mapping to HAM10000 documented, including the classes that do not overlap.
- Fitzpatrick retained as an evaluation axis, never as a model input.

# Data audit — HAM10000 (ISIC collection 212)

Generated by `backend/src/data/audit_collection.py` into
`results/ham10000_audit.json`.

Licence **CC-BY-NC**. 11,720 images · 8,838 lesions · no patient identifiers.
Resolution: width 600–600, median 600.
On-disk size via ISIC: 0.29 GB.

## It is not 10,015 images

The canonical HAM10000 release has 10,015 images. ISIC's collection has **11,720** —
10,015 plus the ISIC 2018 Task 3 validation (193) and test (1,512) sets, whose labels were
released afterwards. Every record here carries a diagnosis.

The extra 1,705 labelled images are usable and we will use them, but the discrepancy is
recorded here so that nobody later compares our numbers against a published HAM10000
result and assumes the same evaluation set. **Our HAM10000 numbers are not comparable to
published HAM10000 numbers**, and any comparison we draw must say so.

## Class distribution

| Diagnosis | Images | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nevus | 7,737 | 66.0% |
| Pigmented benign keratosis | 1,338 | 11.4% |
| Melanoma, NOS | 1,305 | 11.1% |
| Basal cell carcinoma | 622 | 5.3% |
| Squamous cell carcinoma, NOS | 229 | 2.0% |
| Benign soft tissue proliferations - Vascular | 180 | 1.5% |
| Dermatofibroma | 160 | 1.4% |
| Solar or actinic keratosis | 149 | 1.3% |

## Two constraints this imposes

### Splits can only be made at lesion level

PAD-UFES-20 carries `patient_id`; this collection does not — only `lesion_id`
(8,838 of them across 11,720 images). So the finest safe split unit here is the
lesion. That still prevents the worst leak (the same lesion in train and test), but it
cannot prevent two lesions from the same patient being split across folds, because the
data does not say who the patient is.

Recorded as a known limitation rather than discovered later. The audit script reports
`finest_split_unit` for exactly this reason, and the splitter reads it rather than assuming.

### The licences differ, and the stricter one governs

HAM10000 is **CC-BY-NC** — non-commercial. PAD-UFES-20 is **CC-BY**. Any model whose
weights were trained on HAM10000 inherits the non-commercial restriction, which covers
everything in this project since HAM10000 is the pretraining corpus.

This is fine for an academic project and a portfolio piece. It does mean the result cannot
be commercialised without retraining on permissively licensed data, and the model card
must state it.

| PAD-UFES-20 | 2,298 / 2,298 | 238 MB | 0 | 0 |

## The manifest's size field does not describe the file served

ISIC's manifest reports `files.full.size` as a value **exactly 3,209 bytes smaller** than
the object S3 actually returns — the same constant offset for every image in both
collections, confirmed against the server's own `Content-Length`.

The downloads are correct; the metadata is what disagrees. But the first version of the
downloader used that field to decide whether a file was already present, which meant the
comparison never matched: a restart would have re-fetched all 14,018 images, and a
truncated file would have passed as valid.

Completeness is therefore checked by JPEG structure — start-of-image `FFD8` at the front,
end-of-image `FFD9` at the back — which is what actually distinguishes a whole file from a
half-written one. A freshly downloaded file is checked before it is moved into place, so a
failed request can never leave a partial image behind under a real filename.

`backend/tests/test_download.py` pins this: whole JPEG accepted; truncated, empty,
non-JPEG and missing files all rejected.

# Splits

Built by `backend/src/data/split.py`, seed 42, assignments committed in
`data/<name>/splits.json`.

The split unit is read from each collection's audit rather than assumed:
PAD-UFES-20 at **patient** level, HAM10000 at **lesion** level, because that is the
finest unit each dataset's metadata actually supports. Stratified by triage label with
`StratifiedGroupKFold`, so the grouping constraint is respected while class balance is
kept as close as the grouping allows.

## The melanoma count confirms the triage decision

| | train | val | test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PAD-UFES-20 melanoma | 42 | 6 | **4** |
| HAM10000 melanoma | 897 | 235 | 173 |

Four melanoma images in the phone-photo test set. The estimate in ADR 0005 was five; the
real number is four. A per-class melanoma recall computed on four examples moves by 25
percentage points if a single image flips, so it cannot carry a headline — which is
precisely why malignancy triage is the primary task and why melanoma recall is reported
only with its confidence interval and support count beside it.

HAM10000 has 173 melanomas in its own test set, so melanoma performance *is* measurable
in the dermatoscopic domain. That contrast is itself part of the finding: the class that
matters most clinically is the one the deployment domain can say least about.

## Leakage

`backend/tests/test_split.py` asserts that no patient or lesion appears in more than one
split, that every image is assigned exactly once, that all three triage classes appear in
every split, and that re-running the splitter reproduces the committed assignment exactly.
